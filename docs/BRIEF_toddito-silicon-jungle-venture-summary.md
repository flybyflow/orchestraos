# Toddito: The First Proof Case for Silicon Jungle Ventures

*A venture summary, written for review before any external sharing.*

## What Toddito Is

Toddito helps a consultant or a leader understand an organization quickly
and honestly. Instead of a long written survey, a person has a guided
voice conversation — about forty minutes — answering questions about how
their organization actually works today, not how they wish it worked.
From that conversation, Toddito generates a diagnostic report: where the
organization stands, what's working, where the real friction is, and what
that friction tends to cost at this stage of growth.

It was built by Mo Sersouri together with Todd Welch, the person whose
life's work the product is actually built on. Todd spent 38 years
developing and refining this diagnostic method with real consulting
clients, long before Toddito existed in software form. Bringing Todd's
methodology into a modern, voice-led, AI-assisted product is the whole
premise of the venture — not inventing a new idea, but finally building
the right vessel for a proven one.

## Where It Comes From: A 38-Year-Old Idea, Rediscovered

In the course of building Toddito, a genuine piece of history surfaced:
the actual 1987 software Todd originally built this methodology into, on
old floppy disks, still readable today. The product's real name, recovered
directly from the software itself, was **"The Organization Diagnosis
System,"** built by two real, named companies — STSC, Inc. and Dialectics,
Inc. — and nicknamed **THOR**, short for **"The Organization
Reappraisal."**

This matters for more than nostalgia. It confirms that Toddito isn't a
hackathon idea dressed up as a methodology — it's a modern rebuild of a
diagnostic system with a 38-year track record, run on real consulting
engagements, by the same person who originated it. That kind of provenance
is a genuine trust asset with the audience Toddito is built for:
independent consultants and boutique consulting firms who need to
understand a new client's organization fast, and want a tool built on
real, validated methodology rather than a generic personality quiz.

The 1987 system also turned out to be bigger than the version Todd and Mo
rebuilt at the hackathon where Toddito began. It had several different
versions tuned for different kinds of clients, and — genuinely interesting
— a separate module built specifically to assess the trust and credibility
between a consultant and their client, distinct from the core
organizational diagnostic. That's a real, concrete roadmap: it tells us
what a fuller version of Toddito could eventually include, grounded in
what the original system already proved out, not invented from scratch.

## What's Built Today — and What's Still Ahead

Here is the honest picture, not a rounded-up one: Todd's own framework for
this methodology describes **seven distinct products** it could support —
the core organizational diagnostic (built, this is Toddito today), a tool
for aligning executive teams, a self-assessment tool for consultants
themselves, a tool for evaluating cultural fit in mergers and
acquisitions, a due-diligence tool for investors, and two others. **One of
those seven is built. Six are real, documented ideas Todd has described in
detail — not yet built.**

That is not a weakness to hide. It's the actual size of the opportunity:
Toddito today proves the core idea works. The other six products are a
concrete, IP-backed expansion path, not speculation about "what we might
add someday."

## What's Already Working Well

The single most important thing Toddito has already proven: the AI-
generated report itself is genuinely good. This is not a small thing —
most AI-generated business reports read like a machine wrote them:
technical, clinical, generic. Toddito's reports read like something a
skilled human consultant wrote, in language that matches how real
practitioners are trained to talk about organizations, not like a
computer summarizing data. That's the hardest part of this kind of
product to get right, and it's already working, live, today.

## One Thing to Resolve Before This Goes Further

There is one real, unresolved item that should be closed — or at least
squarely understood — before Toddito is shared more broadly or pitched
externally.

Toddito's core interaction is a voice conversation, which means it
records and analyzes a person's actual voice. Voice recordings are
considered sensitive personal data in a way that text answers aren't —
regulators and privacy law treat it differently, closer to how biometric
data (like a fingerprint) is treated. Back in June, when this product
direction was first properly reviewed, this was flagged as the single
most important thing to get right before Toddito is used at any real
scale: the privacy policy and terms of service covering how that voice
data is collected, used, and protected need to be properly in place first.
As of today, that item is still open — it has not yet been finalized.

This isn't a reason not to move forward. It's a reason to sequence
correctly: close this specific item, in parallel with everything else,
before Toddito goes in front of a wide audience or becomes something
people are asked to trust with their voice. Flagging it here plainly,
because a venture summary that quietly leaves this out would be doing
exactly the kind of thing this item exists to prevent.

## Toddito's Role in Silicon Jungle: Venture #1

Inside Silicon Jungle's own model, Toddito isn't just one portfolio
company among many — it's meant to be **the first proof case for the
entire studio.**

Here's the flywheel: Toddito succeeds as a real, revenue-generating
business. Once it does, Toddito itself becomes a sponsor — funding the
next cohort of hackathon ventures that come through Silicon Jungle,
rather than simply graduating and walking away as a portfolio company.
The studio's own event funnel (a weekend build event, followed by a
longer build sprint, followed by a longer incubation period) is the
pipeline that produces the next Toddito. If the flywheel works, Silicon
Jungle doesn't need to keep finding new outside funding for every future
cohort — its own successful ventures start funding the ones behind them.

## The Broader Go-To-Market Thesis: Relationships Before Code

There's a bigger idea behind all of this, in the operator's own words: the
technology moat is gone. Anyone can build software quickly now — that's
no longer the scarce thing. What's actually scarce, and actually valuable,
is trust and relationships: someone who already has 500 real relationships
in an industry is worth more to a venture than an engineer who can build
fast but knows no one.

So the studio's actual bet isn't "recruit the best technical builders." It's
"recruit industry experts who already have trust and customer access — a
hotel operator, a physician, a broker — and pair them with the technical
execution capability the studio supplies." Tulum is the specific place
this starts because Tulum is a place where relationships, hospitality, and
community already work as a way of doing business — before it's ever
tested as a cold digital marketing funnel somewhere else.

## The Thread Connecting Everything

Here's what ties the whole story together, including the parts that
happened before Toddito existed. Going back through the operator's own
history — the 1987 system, and three earlier attempts the operator built
before Toddito, each one aimed at some version of this same problem — every
single one of them was really chasing the same underlying bet:
**technology that makes the space between people — their relationships,
not just their individual data — legible and usable.**

Each earlier attempt got one real piece of that right and then didn't
carry it all the way to a finished, working product. Toddito is the first
version where the whole thing actually works end to end: real data
capture, real synthesis, a real report someone can use immediately. That's
not a small claim — it's the reason this venture is the right one to be
Silicon Jungle's first proof case, rather than an earlier attempt at the
same idea.
