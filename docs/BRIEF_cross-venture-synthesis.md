# How Silicon Jungle, Toddi.to, and Keonda.io Actually Fit Together

  not a status report on each one separately.
- **Rewritten 2026-09-30 to the plain-language standard** (per
  `docs/BRIEF_how-we-come-across-plain.md`): same facts, same flags, none of the
  numbered-section framework the first draft used. Nothing here was wrong before —
  it just read like a report instead of an answer.
- **What this still is:** a map of what depends on what, and where the real risk

## The short version

You asked how six things fit together — Silicon Jungle, Buildathon Experiences,
Agentic Product Lab, the Incubator, Toddi.to, Keonda.io. They're really two things.

Silicon Jungle is one studio, running one funnel, and every other name on that
list except Toddi.to and Keonda is just that same studio described from a
different angle. Toddi.to is a separate, older project that Silicon Jungle didn't
create — but the two of them aren't as separate as they first looked, because
Toddi.to's own biggest leap forward happened at one of Silicon Jungle's events.
Keonda is doing two real jobs inside Silicon Jungle at once, and one of those jobs
already had a real privacy scare, caught and fixed once, on the same kind of
system Silicon Jungle is being careful about reusing.

## Silicon Jungle, Buildathon, Agentic Product Lab, and the Incubator are one thing

Silicon Jungle Experience is the studio — live today at `sje.ploy.build`, with
`siliconjungle.io` sitting reserved as the brand name (the email works, there's no
site there yet). Mo runs it, with Stephan Mai and Richard Rygg.

Buildathon Experiences is very likely just the event name for what Silicon
Jungle's own funnel calls "The Weekender" — a 48-hour weekend where people form
teams and build something real. Eight of these have run so far, with a ninth
already being planned. The two descriptions match closely enough that they're
almost certainly the same thing under two names, though that's worth a direct
one-line confirmation rather than treating it as settled.

"Agentic Product Lab" doesn't show up anywhere as its own separate initiative —
the document this all came from is literally titled "Silicon Jungle as an Agentic
Product Lab Platform." Read plainly, that phrase is just the strategy: giving
Weekender teams access to the same planning-and-review process this fleet already
uses internally, so a founder stops having to personally sit with every team. This
is the one real naming question in the whole picture — nothing found so far
distinguishes it from Silicon Jungle's own platform strategy, but it's still worth
asking directly rather than assuming.

And the Incubator is just the funnel's third and final stage — sixteen weeks,
cross-domain teams building the company together, the on-ramp into what's called
"Silicon Ventures." There's already one real graduate: Snap Eats, a food app where
the technical side owns the build and the outside partners own getting it to
market. The operator's own "Industry Expert Venture Studio" thesis — turning
people who already have customers and trust into founders, by handing them
technology as leverage — is the business model description of this whole studio,
not a separate fifth thing sitting next to it.

Put together: one studio, one funnel (Weekender, which is probably Buildathon,
leading to the Experience, leading to the Incubator), one business model, and one
platform strategy for scaling it. Four names, very likely one thing seen from
different angles — worth the operator saying so directly before anyone builds
against four separate initiatives that don't actually exist.

## Toddi.to is a different, older thing — but not as separate as it looks

Toddi.to, which is migrating its branding over from `getyourpulse.io`, comes from
somewhere else entirely: Todd's own diagnostic methodology, carried through three
earlier versions called Koherent, and checked against a 1987 piece of software
that did something similar on paper. None of that has anything to do with Silicon
Jungle technically. It's being brought into Silicon Jungle's world as something to
show sponsors, alongside Shaw Cole's own project, ListMagic.

But that's an understatement, and the operator corrected it directly: Toddito's
own biggest leap forward didn't happen alone. It happened at a buildathon,
specifically because people pulled together in a room and got it done as a group.
That's not a future hope — it already happened. Which means the loop already runs
in one direction (Silicon Jungle feeding Toddi.to), on top of the separate,
forward-looking idea that once Toddi.to makes real money, it commits some of that
back to sponsoring future Silicon Jungle weekends — or offers its own diagnostic
tool as something teams use at the start of a weekend, the same instinct behind
Keonda's dinner-seating work below. That second part is still just a proposal,
not something built or committed to yet. Two more examples of the same loop, per
the operator: Keonda itself is meant to support the whole ecosystem once it's
further along, and separately, Paul Schwartz's work is already running real
diagnostics for people at these events today, not proposed, live.

Put together, this is really the operator's own definition of what Silicon Jungle
is: things built here grow up and feed back into what built them. See
`docs/BRIEF_silicon-jungle-the-why.md` for the fuller, plainer version of that
reframing.

## Keonda is doing two jobs, and one of them comes with a real warning

Keonda isn't one thing wearing two names by accident — the operator confirmed
directly that it's one system doing two jobs.

The first job is remembering people. It's the relationship-graph engine behind
"Gigi's dinner" — figuring out who to seat next to whom at an event, and why,
based on who can actually help whom. This is described as the thing that turns
"came to one weekend" into "got invited back" — without it, there's no real
retention loop underneath the funnel at all. A redesign of how this looks has
already been reviewed and approved; the actual code for the missing piece (a
privacy-safe way of explaining a seating choice to a host) hasn't been written
yet.

The second job is running the events themselves. Since the seventh Buildathon, a
human still runs each event, but Keonda now helps as an AI co-facilitator, and by
the eighth event the operator described being noticeably less hands-on.

Here's the part worth being careful about: Silicon Jungle's own WhatsApp presence
already runs on this same shared Keonda system today, deliberately limited to
watching and letting a human broadcast, nothing automatic. That same system has a
real, already-fixed privacy leak in its own history — a search once returned
someone else's community's private records before it was caught and patched. This
isn't a new risk being raised for the first time. It's proof that the exact kind
of risk Silicon Jungle is already being careful about, when it comes to giving
outside attendees any kind of shared access, has actually happened once, on a
system right next door, and was caught.

## One more thing that lined up on its own

A completely separate piece of research done the same night, for an unrelated
question about voice-agent costs, ran into three other repos in Silicon Jungle's
GitHub org and found they're a real, working product: an astrology-companion app,
persona named Luna, with its own voice pipeline and admin dashboard. Silicon
Jungle's own list of hackathon-born ventures already named "Kokoro" as one of
these, without being sure of the exact order. The two findings back each other up
without either one knowing about the other — Kokoro is a real venture in the same
family as Snap Eats and this very fleet's own codebase (which also started as a
hackathon project), not a loose thread.

## What's actually independent

Strip away the sponsor idea and the sponsor-showcase strategy, and Toddi.to's
actual technology owes nothing to Silicon Jungle, Keonda, or the Buildathon
funnel. It would keep existing exactly as it is if Silicon Jungle didn't. The
connection between them lives entirely in strategy and history now — a shared
founder, a proposed funding idea, one real weekend where the two crossed paths —
not in any shared code.

## What's still worth asking directly

Two things in here are inference, not confirmed fact, and both are cheap to
settle with one question: whether "Agentic Product Lab" really is just another
name for the platform strategy, and whether Buildathon and The Weekender really
are the same event series under two names. Neither one changes the shape of the
map much either way — they're just worth saying out loud rather than assuming.

One more thing, stated plainly rather than glossed over: nothing here suggests
the same work is being built twice. The community/retention work on Keonda and
the platform-access work on Silicon Jungle are both being handled with the same
patience — hold off on the harder infrastructure until there's real usage data
that says it's needed.
